\section{Part C: Normal Approximation via CLT}
\begin{itemize}
    \item By the Central Limit Theorem, the distributions of sample means should approximate normal distributions. Determine the parameters (mean and standard deviation) of the approximating normal distributions for both power law and uniform cases.
    \item Overlay the probability density functions (PDFs) of these normal distributions on the corresponding plot from part B.
\end{itemize}
-----\\

I computed the sample mean and the standard deviation of the sample means for each set of replicates, and then overlaid the PDF of the corresponding Gaussian defined with these (mean, std.) pairs on top of each of their respective plots from part B.\\

According to the Central Limit Theorem, for samples of size $n$ drawn from a distribution with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, the distribution of sample means should approximate a normal distribution with mean $\mu$ and standard error $\text{SE} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$.\\

For the Uniform(0.25, 1.25) distribution, the theoretical parameters are:
$$\mu = \frac{0.25 + 1.25}{2} = 0.75$$
$$\sigma = \frac{1.25 - 0.25}{\sqrt{12}} \approx 0.289$$
$$\text{SE} = \frac{0.289}{\sqrt{100}} \approx 0.029$$

And the Pareto(a=2.3) distribution:
$$\mu = \frac{1}{a-1} = \frac{1}{1.3} \approx 0.769$$
$$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{(a-1)^2(a-2)} = \frac{1}{(1.3)^2(0.3)} \approx 1.97 \implies \sigma \approx 1.40$$
$$\text{SE} = \frac{1.40}{\sqrt{100}} \approx 0.140$$

The observed parameters from the sample means for each replicate size are shown below:

For the Uniform distribution, the observed standard deviation stabilizes around 0.029, matching the theoretical standard error. For the Pareto distribution, the observed standard deviation increases from 0.121 to 0.176 as we get more replicates. The initial underestimation with 10 replicates likely occurs because we don't have enough samples to capture the full variability from the heavy tail, whereas the 1000 replicates provide a more realistic understanding of the variability caused by the ocassional extremes. The observed means also converge: the Uniform mean reaches exactly 0.750 with 1000 replicates, and the Pareto mean reaches 0.775, fairly close to the theoretical 0.769.\\

This demonstrates that while the theoretical distribution of sample means is fixed (determined by the original distribution and sample size $n=100$), we need a sufficiently high number of replicates to accurately estimate its parameters, especially for heavy-tailed distributions like the Pareto.\\

The function for computing and overlaying the normal PDFs is shown below:

\begin{verbatim}
def overlay_normal(sample_means: np.ndarray, dist_name: str, 
                       replicate_size: int, output_filename: str):
    """
    Plots the histogram of sample means with overlaid normal PDF.
    
    :param sample_means: Array of sample means
    :type sample_means: np.ndarray
    :param dist_name: Name of the distribution
    :type dist_name: str
    :param replicate_size: Replicate size
    :type replicate_size: int
    :param output_filename: Output filename for figure
    :type output_filename: str
    """
    sns.set_style("whitegrid")
    sns.set_palette("muted")
    plt.figure(figsize=(10, 6))

    ax = sns.histplot(sample_means, bins=50, stat='density',
                      edgecolor='white', alpha=0.7, label='Sample Mean Distribution')

    mu = np.mean(sample_means)
    sigma = np.std(sample_means)
    x = np.linspace(min(sample_means), max(sample_means), 500)
    pdf = stats.norm.pdf(x, loc=mu, scale=sigma)
    
    plt.plot(x, pdf, 'r--', label=f'Normal Approx. PDF\nmu={mu:.3f}, sigma={sigma:.3f}')
    
    ax.set_xlabel('Sample Mean', fontsize=14, labelpad=15)
    ax.set_ylabel('Density', fontsize=14, labelpad=15)
    ax.set_title(f'Normal Overlay for {dist_name} Sample Means of Replicate Size {replicate_size})',
                 fontsize=16, pad=20)
    
    plt.legend(fontsize=11)
    plt.tight_layout()
    os.makedirs("../figures", exist_ok=True)
    plt.savefig(f"../figures/{output_filename}", bbox_inches='tight', dpi=300)
\end{verbatim}
